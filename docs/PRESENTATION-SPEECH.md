# UC Admissions Dashboard Judge Speech

## Two-minute version

Hello, we studied how representation changes across three stages of the UC
admissions pipeline: applying, being admitted, and enrolling.

Our question was: among UC freshman applicants from 2017 through 2025, how did
application share, admission rate, and enrollment yield change across the race
and ethnicity categories reported by UC, and how did those patterns differ by
campus and year?

We calculated every percentage directly from counts. Application share is a
group's applicants divided by all applicants in the same scope. Admission rate
is admits divided by applicants. Yield is enrollees divided by admits. We never
average percentages, replace missing values with zero, or add campuses to
recreate the supplied Systemwide total.

The first important result is that these three metrics tell different stories.
From 2017 to 2025, Asian application share had the largest increase, rising 3.42
percentage points, while White application share decreased 4.69 points.
Admission rates rose for every reported category, but by very different
amounts: the largest increase was 22.93 points for Pacific Islander applicants,
while the International category increased only 1.88 points.

Enrollment yield moved differently. American Indian yield increased 4.12
points, while Hispanic and Latino yield decreased 13.55 points. That matters
because admission does not automatically translate into enrollment.

The dashboard lets us move from the Systemwide story to a selected metric,
group, year, and campus. Counts stay beside rates, so a large percentage change
for a small group is not presented without its denominator.

We also included a separate GPA and field-of-study context. For fall 2025, users
can examine broad freshman disciplines or named Berkeley transfer majors and
compare a GPA with the supplied admitted-student percentile range. We keep this
separate from ethnicity because these files cannot be joined at the individual
level. It is historical context, not an admission prediction.

Our conclusion is not that race or ethnicity caused any outcome. This data is
aggregated and does not control for preparation, major, residency, or applicant
choices. What it does show clearly is that application representation,
admission, and enrollment are distinct stages, and understanding UC outcomes
requires looking at all three rather than a single admission-rate number.

Thank you. We're happy to demonstrate any campus, group, year, or metric.

## If a judge asks about fairness

Say: "The dashboard identifies descriptive differences in aggregate outcomes.
It does not establish why those differences exist or make a fairness verdict.
That would require applicant-level variables and a validated causal design that
this dataset does not provide."

## If a judge asks whether GPA predicts admission

Say: "No. We show supplied historical GPA ranges for compatible fall 2025
discipline or transfer-major groups. Comparing a GPA to that range does not
produce an individual probability."
